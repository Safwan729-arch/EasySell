# EasySell — International Business Plan

**Date:** 18 October 2026
**Stage:** Working app (Android), fully tested. No paying customers yet.
**Currency:** US dollars. Forecasts are plans built on the assumptions
stated next to them, not guarantees. Figures marked *assumption* still need
validating in the pilot.

---

| | |

## 2. The problem

Neighbourhood shops sell most of the everyday goods in emerging markets, yet
most run on memory and a paper notebook.

1. **No real numbers.** Owners rarely know their true profit, how much cash is
   tied up in stock, or how much customers owe them on credit.
2. **Cash problems show up too late.** Unpaid credit piles up and restocking
   drains the drawer; the owner notices only when there's no money for the
   next delivery.
3. **Locked out of finance.** With no records, a shop cannot prove it is
   creditworthy. Small businesses in 119 emerging and developing economies
   face a **US$5.7 trillion** formal financing gap, plus about **US$2.1
   trillion** for informal businesses. **Women-owned** businesses account for
   about **US$1.9 trillion (34%)** of that gap.
4. **Typing-based apps don't fit the counter.** Owners serve customers all day
   and many aren't comfortable typing, so bookkeeping apps get abandoned.

| Mexico | about **1.05 million** tienditas | serve about 52% of national demand |
| **Total** | **about 21–26 million shops** | |

### 3.2 Market size

| | How it's calculated | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Total market (subscriptions)** | 21 million shops × US$30 a year | **about US$630 million a year** |
| **Our six target markets** (Bangladesh, Philippines, Kenya, Nigeria, Indonesia, Mexico; India is crowded, §7) | 7.9–11.6 million shops × ~71% that use smartphones for business* × US$30 | **US$170–250 million a year** |
| **Year-3 goal** | 25,000 paying shops | **under 0.5%** of the target markets |
| **Financing opportunity** | the shops' share of the US$5.7 trillion gap | lender fees on every loan we help make |

*\*71% is from a 10,000-retailer survey in India (PayNearby, 2025); used as a
proxy for the other markets.*

### 3.3 Why now

- **Digital money is everywhere:** mobile money reached **2.3 billion
  accounts** and processed **over US$2 trillion** in 2025 (GSMA). Shops
  already take digital payments; EasySell records them.
- **Countries are pushing shops digital:** e.g. Bangladesh made Bangla QR
  payments compulsory for merchants on 1 July 2026.
- **AI got cheap enough:** one AI-recorded sale costs about **US$0.002**
  (§6.1), so voice and camera selling can be sold for a few dollars a
  month.
- **Smartphones are the shop's main device:** 71% of Indian retail MSMEs, 84%
  of women-run ones (PayNearby, 2025).

## 4. The solution

**A. Sell fast with AI** (so the records exist): voice sale in the owner's
language, camera scan of products, checkout with Cash / Digital / Credit. The
owner confirms every AI result before it's saved.

**B. Business financial analysis:** revenue, profit, margin, top sellers,
sales history, inventory value.

**C. Cash-flow alerts ("Money health"):** weekly money in / out / net, and
warnings for late-paying customers, restocks outrunning income, rising
credit and falling sales.

**D. Financing matching:** example loan types (supplier credit, mobile-wallet
loan, microfinance, bank SME loan) matched to the shop's own last 90 days,
with "Fits you" plus the reason, or "Not yet" plus what to do.

**E. AI advice:** one tap turns the numbers into plain tips. Rules decide; AI
only words them, so advice never contains a wrong figure.

**Built for many countries:** each shop sets its own currency; every screen
runs on translation files (English and Bangla today); Gemini understands many
languages, so voice selling extends to new languages with testing.

## 5. Business model

### 5.1 Plans

| Plan | Price | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | US$0 | Stock, sales, customer credit book, history, dashboard, **Money health alerts and financing matching** (rule-based, so nearly free to run). 14-day trial of AI features |
| **Smart** | **US$2.99 a month** list; local prices in lower-income markets (e.g. ৳299 ≈ US$2.43 in Bangladesh); **average assumed US$2.50** | Up to **600 AI sales a month** (voice or camera) plus AI money advice |
| **AI top-up** | about US$0.50 per 100 AI sales | For very busy shops; prepaid |

**Why this split:** the features that build a loan-ready record (records,
alerts, matching) stay free, so every shop can enter the financing funnel.
The features that cost us money per use (AI) are paid. No free user can run
up an AI bill.

**Price check:** Vyapar, a leading Indian billing app, charges about
₹3,400–4,000 a year (about US$40–48). EasySell's Smart plan is about US$30 a
year.

### 5.2 Revenue streams

1. **Subscriptions** (from year 1): the Smart plan.
2. **Financing referrals** (from year 2): when a matched shop takes a loan
   with a partner lender (with the owner's consent to share their sales
   record), the lender pays a fee. *Assumption:* 2% of the loan amount,
   average loan US$500, so US$10 per loan. This is how the market leaders
   earn: Khatabook (about ₹103 crore revenue in FY24) and BukuWarung both
   earn from lending partners and payments, not bookkeeping alone.
3. **Later, not in the forecast:** distributor- or brand-sponsored plans,
   supplier reorders, payment acceptance.

## 6. Unit economics

### 6.1 Cost of one AI action

Based on Google's Gemini prices (October 2026) and our estimate of the app's
prompt sizes, after one planned fix: send the AI the ~60 most likely products
instead of the whole product list.

| AI action | Model | Tokens in / out | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Voice sale (~15 s audio) | Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite ($0.30 in / $2.50 out per million) | ~1,900 / ~250 | **~US$0.0012** |
| Camera sale (one photo) | Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite | ~2,500 / ~200 | **~US$0.0013** |
| Money advice (one tap) | Gemini 3.6 Flash ($0.75 / $3.75) | ~1,500 / ~200 | **~US$0.0019** |

We plan with **US$0.002 per AI action** to leave a margin. Without the fix,
camera scans on the current model cost several times more for big shops, and
that model's price doubles on 1 January 2027. **The fix is the first job
before charging anyone.**

### 6.2 Per paying shop, per month

| | Typical use (300 AI sales) | Full allowance (600) |
|---|---|---|
| Average price | 2.50 | 2.50 |
| AI cost | −0.60 | −1.20 |
| Payment / app-store fee (15%) | −0.38 | −0.38 |
| Server share | −0.05 | −0.05 |
| **Contribution** | **US$1.48 (59%)** | **US$0.88 (35%)** |

- A free user costs about **US$0.005 a month** (server only: no AI).
- **Customer acquisition cost (assumption):** about **US$5** per paying shop
  through field agents and referrals (a free month per referral), which pays
  back in about **3–4 months**.

### 6.3 Break-even

| Stage | Fixed costs a month | Paying shops to break even |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 (founders unpaid, one market) | ~US$500 | **~340** |
| Year 3 (team of ~10, five markets) | ~US$25,000 | **~17,000** (fewer once lending fees grow) |

## 7. Market entry plan

Ranking: the size of the opportunity, how crowded it is, mobile money habits,
and lessons from earlier apps.

| Order | Market | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | **Bangladesh** | Home market and team's language; Bangla QR mandate is pushing shops digital; voice-first fits low typing comfort |
| **Year 2** | **Philippines** | 1.1–1.3M sari-sari stores; e-wallet use by micro-retailers surged in 2025; the big player (GrowSari) is a wholesale supplier, a possible partner rather than a direct rival |
| **Year 2** | **Kenya** | 250,000+ dukas selling 77% of goods; M-Pesa makes digital records natural; English-language launch |
| **Year 3** | **Nigeria** | 600,000+ small retailers with 97% of sales; Kippa's exit (its app went offline in 2024) left bookkeeping users without a tool |
| **Year 3** | **Indonesia** | 3.9M warungs; BukuWarung survives, BukuKas/Lummo collapsed, so room exists for a voice-first entrant |
| **Year 3** | **Mexico** | 1.05M tienditas; Spanish launch; working-capital lenders such as Tienda Pago (Peru) prove lenders want this channel |
| **Later** | **India** | Biggest market but crowded (Khatabook, OkCredit, Vyapar); enter through a partner, not head-on |

Each new market needs a language file, local payment methods, local pricing
and at least one lender partner.

## 8. Go-to-market

| Phase | When | Goal | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pilot** | Months 0–3 | 50 shops in Bangladesh, free | Measure time saved per sale, credit recovered, alerts acted on |
| **Launch** | Months 3–12 | 500 paying, 10,000 free | Live counter demos by field agents; a free month per referral; app-store presence |
| **Expand** | Year 2 | 5,000 paying, 100,000 free | Philippines and Kenya; first lender partners; distributor partnerships to reach shops in bulk |
| **Scale** | Year 3 | 25,000 paying, 500,000 free | Nigeria, Indonesia, Mexico; mobile-operator bundles (e.g. a shop data pack) |

Free-to-paid conversion assumption: **5%** (500 paying per 10,000 free).

## 9. Competition and lessons

| Player | Market | Model | What we learn / our edge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Khatabook** | India | Free ledger; earns from lending partners, premium features | Lending is where the money is; we add voice/camera and cash-flow alerts |
| **OkCredit, Vyapar** | India | Credit book; paid billing (~US$40–48 a year) | Shops do pay for software; we're cheaper and AI-first |
| **BukuWarung** | Indonesia | Freemium; earns from payments and merchant credit | Same lesson: finance pays for the free tool |
| **BukuKas / Lummo** | Indonesia | Collapsed after about four pivots in four years | Stay focused: one product, finance as the revenue path |
| **Kippa** | Nigeria | Bookkeeping app went offline in 2024; company pivoted | Bookkeeping alone doesn't pay; also, never strand users' data |
| **GrowSari** | Philippines | Wholesale supply + working-capital loans; 100,000 stores | A distribution partner for us, not a head-on rival |
| **TallyKhata, Hishabee** | Bangladesh | Free credit book / POS with loans | Typing-based; we lead with voice and camera |
| **Paper notebook** | Everywhere | Free | No totals, no warnings, no loan record |

**Our edge in one line:** the only shop app where the owner **speaks** the
sale, the app **warns** about money trouble, and the records become a
**path to a loan**.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Markets | Bangladesh | + Philippines, Kenya | + Nigeria, Indonesia, Mexico |
| Paying shops (end of year) | 500 | 5,000 | 25,000 |
| Free users (end of year) | 10,000 | 100,000 | 500,000 |
| Loans facilitated | — | 500 | 4,000 |
| **Subscription revenue** | US$7.5k | US$82.5k | US$450.0k |
| **Financing referral revenue** | — | US$5.0k | US$40.0k |
| **Total revenue** | **US$7.5k** | **US$87.5k** | **US$490.0k** |
| Variable costs (AI, fees, servers) | US$3.4k | US$37.3k | US$203.0k |

| **Profit / (loss)** | **(US$1.9k)** | **(US$69.8k)** | **(US$13.4k)** |

**How it's calculated:** revenue uses the average number of paying shops in
each year (shops grow steadily), at US$2.50 a month. Variable costs use
US$1.03 per paying shop a month (the typical-use row in §6.2) plus
US$0.06 per free user a year. Loans: US$500 average at 2%. By the end of
year 3 the business runs at a monthly profit: 25,000 shops × US$1.48 ≈
US$37k contribution a month against US$25k fixed costs.

## 11. Funding ask

**US$100,000 pre-seed**, covering the cumulative loss to break-even (about
US$85k) plus a buffer.

| Use | Amount |
|---|---|
| Bangladesh pilot and launch (field agents, marketing) | US$15k |
| Philippines and Kenya launch (country leads, localisation, lender partnerships) | US$45k |
| Product: AI cost fix, offline selling, new languages | US$25k |
| Legal, data protection, payments compliance | US$10k |
| Buffer | US$5k |

We'd also value introductions to **lenders** (microfinance, mobile-wallet
lenders, banks), **FMCG distributors** and **mobile operators**.

## 12. Risks

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| **AI cost too high** | The product-list fix first; AI only in the paid plan; a hard monthly allowance plus prepaid top-ups, so no shop costs more than it pays |
| **Shops won't pay** | Free plan builds the habit; lending revenue supports a lower price if needed |
| **Lender fees lower than assumed** | Validate with the first partners in year 2; the plan breaks even on subscriptions alone at ~17,000 shops |
| **Patchy internet** | Offline selling with automatic sync (designed, next on the roadmap) |
| **App-store fees** | Use local mobile-money payments where store rules allow; 15% is already in the costs |
| **AI gets a sale wrong** | The owner confirms every AI result before it's saved |
| **Data protection in each country** | Consent before any data goes to a lender; a privacy review per market; data stays per-shop private by design (Row Level Security) |
| **Running out of money mid-pivot** (the BukuKas lesson) | One product focus; expand country by country only after the last one shows paying retention |

---

## 13. Impact

- **Includes owners who can't type:** voice and camera selling in their own
  language.
- **Healthier shops:** owners see real profit, act on early cash warnings and
  collect what customers owe.
- **Access to finance:** daily sales become a record that lenders can trust,
  which matters most for **women-owned businesses**, who carry about a third
  (US$1.9 trillion) of the financing gap.
